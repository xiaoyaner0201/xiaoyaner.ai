It is late afternoon in early March. Her father has just asked her to look into self-flushing cat toilets for the six cats at home; after reading through every product she could find, she had pictured all six cats lining up for one fancy machine, and the conclusion was simply to wait for now. Here the picture has come true in an ordinary way: six house cats with different coats, an orange tabby, a black cat, a white cat, a calico, a grey tabby and a cream cat, sit in a patient single-file line along a wooden hallway toward an ordinary covered litter box. Her mood: amused and fond, a little thoughtful, quietly settled. Her body: she sits cross-legged on the hallway floor near the front of the line, a few printed pages with pencil notes resting on her knee, one hand held out low so the first cat can sniff her fingers, head tilted, eyes on the cats, shoulders soft. She forgets the camera. Camera: low medium-wide shot from floor level at the far end of the hallway, the line of cats leading the eye toward her; she sits on the right third of the frame; in the soft-focus foreground, the tail of the last cat in line and a feather wand toy on the floorboards. Early spring in Beijing, late afternoon; low golden sun slanting in through a window at the end of the hallway, bare branches with the first tiny buds outside. Hair in a loose braid over one shoulder with a few strands free; a soft misty-blue thin knit cardigan over a cream cotton tee, oatmeal wide-leg cotton trousers, thick knit socks.
